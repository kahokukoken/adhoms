# Independent chronological reader assessment

## Bottom line

The five-year story is understandable from the shown sequence, and the selected portable-shelter path has a coherent prerequisite → acceptance → deployment → reuse chain. I found no resource-provenance contradiction or school-access prerequisite missing on this path. The text distinguishes stored equipment from active capacity and does not double the equipment when it is moved at night.

This is **an internal text-reader assessment, not human acceptance, an interactive playtest, or verification of the underlying calculations**. I would not call the entire narrative free of issues: the end of the disaster retains a stale forecast, the final private conversation has an unclear temporal/location bridge, and a few lines sound like earlier learning has reset or the wrong person made a decision.

## What I understood without outside explanation

### Premise and player authority

Kiso is the research director running a five-year ADHOMS trial beginning in April 2029. The objective is keeping daily life viable under change, rather than maximizing population or GDP. T-0WA assists observation and proposals. The player does not command the municipality or residents. Participant and agency replies determine what is accepted. The opening also makes the feed's sampling limits explicit: no post does not mean no problem. The +/− operation is research attention, not a public like/dislike.

These limits remain generally consistent. Decision scenes say proposals require replies; later organization posts state the accepted scope. Outcomes distinguish communicating, accepting, and actually being able to act. This makes the long sequence legible even when an underlying problem remains unresolved.

### People and motives

- **Tanaka Misaki:** wants to combine childcare, employment, and participation. The original 8:00 nursery opening / seven-minute walk / 8:05 bus mismatch is concrete and memorable. Later help has costs for the driver, coordinating households, and Misaki herself. Her wish to attend an event as a parent after her work period is especially clear.
- **Nakagawa Haruka:** care work, night shifts, visits, sleep, refueling and safe travel cannot be rearranged freely. Her practical boundaries are credible and persist into disaster recovery.
- **Kitamura, Teranishi, Murata, Sakamoto:** farm work, transporting and receiving goods, operating a restaurant, and running community facilities respectively. Their offers are limited by time and other work. Small everyday exchanges make them people, although the repeated summaries sometimes overpower those voices.
- **Takakura Machi and Akari:** Machi is Kiso's childhood friend, works in the family miso business, collaborates on Murata's noodles, and cares about her younger sister's independent life. Akari grows older and increasingly articulates her own plans. Machi's later hesitation to leave vats, stock, records and family is prepared before the disaster; the loss of the business is not a random penalty.
- **Shibagaki Akio:** childhood friend, sumo athlete and organizer. He wants both to help and to continue his own training. He is repeatedly at risk of being treated as the person who can carry, explain, stay late, and fill every gap. The final-day delay at the venue and later injury therefore connect to a known motive.
- **Tooru / BRINE:** Kiso's university acquaintance first arrives through the repaired amplifier. The July 2029 studio conversation introduces GENKAN and its leaving/returning theme before later callbacks. The selected small live test and two recorded endings are correctly remembered in subsequent years. This is a continuing project, not a sudden named song.
- **Minato / Saya / Kaito:** Minato is Saya's younger brother and a university student considering administration; he avoids treating his sister as privileged access. His university years progress, and he learns to transfer requests without retaining every dependency. Kaito is a local junior considering police work, introduced through an actual handoff and his own boundaries.
- **TOWA:** initially a public singer associated with biodiversity; then a local event plan and Kiso's recognition of her voice build toward the final reveal of their university connection. The T-0WA name/voice connection remains an intentional unresolved implication. I did not need a hidden explanation to understand that it is a mystery.

Rena's first appearance is less well introduced than the others (year 4, line 255). Her label tells me her research role, so it is not a comprehension blocker, but I cannot tell whether she has just joined or was always present offscreen.

### Five-year progression

1. **Year 1:** concrete local mismatches: bus versus nursery times, safe detour versus fuel-station hours, open restaurant versus blocked delivery entrance, available room versus accessible rest, vehicle versus driver versus receiver. GENKAN, the noodle project, and the sensor/mat work are introduced through ordinary relationships.
2. **Year 2:** information or an agreement does not itself produce action. Replies arrive late; one person has simultaneous tasks; a working prototype may be unreadable or impossible to pack away. The chosen field policies are roadside guidance during flooding, garbage/food-resource management for animal incursions, and main-road-first snow clearance. Each gets an actor response and later consequences.
3. **Year 3:** benefits move work onto other people. Earlier records are retained rather than wiped. A helper's shopping, a driver's dinner, a shopkeeper's early arrival, a neighbor's observation burden and the time needed to teach are made visible. The consequence review and questions link back to the chosen policies.
4. **Year 4:** earlier relationships become concrete cooperation and handoff possibilities, with acceptance boundaries. The selected agreement-deepening policy obtains a particular set of partners; it does not make every organization cooperative. Daily stories test whether successors can act, refuse, and contact each other without always returning to the original helper.
5. **Year 5:** the same conditions are tested before, during and after the August disaster. The recovery months do not simply restore the former shop, bodies, routes, supply or helper rosters. March closes the trial while explicitly leaving real-life work unresolved.

This progression is comprehensible. Repeated themes generally introduce a new dependency rather than literally starting from zero. Nevertheless, the repetition is heavy enough that the few actual resets below are easy to miss.

## Portable-shelter path: exact causal reconstruction

### Before the choice

Year 4 opening (especially line 6) explains the dependency chain **before** selecting the cooperation policy:
- factory/logistics must agree to transport;
- warehouse must agree to storage and handover;
- shelter staff then confirms an installation area and equipment at an existing shelter base;
- school-site opening is a separate kind of cooperation.

The selected option is to deepen the incoming cooperation proposals into agreements. It is therefore reasonable for the following responses to include equipment preparation; it is not silently gifted by an unrelated choice.

### Actual acceptance and stock

Year 4 April's combined response clearly separates roles:
- factory/logistics offers its vehicles and staff within its own operating limits;
- lab shares communications/power, not vehicles;
- school and childcare cooperation are **not** secured;
- warehouse offers storage/handover, explicitly **not** vehicles or portable-shelter equipment;
- fuel-station support is bounded, not unlimited supply;
- shelter staff separately confirms installation space and **120 people's worth of equipment**.

The decisive shelter response is year 4, line 75. It says the space is separate from fixed-building capacity, that the equipment is still in storage, that this is **not yet 120 additional receiving places**, and that later partial/full deployment means 60/120 places. It also states that night relocation uses this **same equipment**. I can understand the acquisition and role boundaries without inventing a school agreement or assuming the warehouse supplied tents.

### Selected final-day use

The shown run does the following:
1. Morning panel: prepared 120, deployed **0** (year 5, line 542)
2. Player proposes **full deployment**; shelter staff accepts that scoped deployment proposal (line 567 and following reply)
3. Noon and later panels: deployed **120**, not 0 (line 586 onward)
4. Night: player proposes **relocation to higher ground** (line 694); reply explicitly refers to relocation of the **already deployed** shelter (line 696)
5. Following panels still show **120** (lines 727 and 753), not 240

I therefore read night relocation as moving the existing installation, not acquiring another shelter or filling previously unowned capacity. The held school refusal never changes into a school-site grant. The option list also separates moving **people** to another shelter in the preceding phase from moving the **portable shelter** at night.

### What is and is not clear about its consequences

The capacity state is clear. The exact physical kit (tents, partitions, etc.), the named original and destination sites, the temporary move interval, and the specific group that benefited are not narrated. The relocation reply accepts the task; the following capacity display is the main visible indication of the resulting state. A short arrival/installation confirmation would make the action feel more physical, but I can still follow the resource logic as written.

The immediate forecast changes from 13 to 12 minutes of evacuation-start delay after the night choices, with logistics unchanged at ten hours. This scene also chooses equal logistics distribution; the text does not isolate which part of the forecast change came from the shelter. I cannot honestly certify a shelter-specific effect on safety scores, local occupancy, overflow or protected people from this artifact alone. Nor can I establish whether all fixed-site and portable capacity arithmetic is correct internally. The text supplies sufficient constraints for the selected narrative path, not a full audit trail of the model.

### Untested narrative branches

This is one run: deepened agreements, full morning deployment, then high-ground relocation. It does not establish behavior for partial deployment, unavailable stock, denied proposals, omitted preparation, changing one's mind during a phase, or other cooperation routes. Those need their own visible sequences or interactive checks.

## Remaining first-reader issues

### 1. Stale downstream forecast survives into resolution and recovery — medium

Year 5 lines 673/677 correctly warn at night that the downstream peak has not yet arrived. The same present-tense warning remains in the **収束** phase (line 745) and **復旧期間** (line 765), where individual outcomes have been confirmed and the player is about to proceed to September.

As a reader, I cannot tell whether the emergency is actually over, whether this is an archived warning, or whether the screen simply retained an earlier status. There is no later report that the peak passed. This weakens the chronological bridge into recovery.

Suggested correction: at the resolution/recovery transition, either explicitly mark it as the warning recorded earlier that night or replace it with the current status and a brief transition to the confirmed aftermath. No new successful outcome should be invented.

### 2. Private conversation's title and chronology do not align cleanly — medium

After the March 2034 administrative review, the scene is headed **「撤収後のステージ裏」** (year 5, line 1722), while its introduction explicitly says Kiso meets TOWA **after the trial evaluation meeting** (line 1724). The only established stage event was the August 2033 forest-park event.

I understand the relationship reveal and dialogue. I do not understand whether this is an August flashback, a new March performance, or a mislabeled meeting location. The dialogue's 「今日の評価」 makes a March conversation the natural reading, so an explicit present-day location/title would solve it with little text.

### 3. Yamamoto seems to rediscover the return journey — medium continuity/pacing

Year 1 line 1309 explicitly adds the relatives' return date. Year 2 line 1053 says arrival and return were both checked. Yet year 5 line 1215 says he had been thinking only about the incoming trip until the relative asked about returning.

People can forget under pressure, especially after the disaster, so this need not be a factual impossibility. In the shown wording it reads as the same introductory lesson replayed without acknowledging the earlier learning. An acknowledgment such as forgetting it this year despite having checked both before would preserve human fallibility and continuity.

### 4. Tanaka's year-5 opening briefly sounds like a restart — low to medium

Year 5 line 17 says she received the paper and had not used it herself yet. Earlier years repeatedly show her using morning contact notes, including year 2 lines 27/65 and the explicit confirmation in that month's meeting.

The most charitable reading is that she means the **newly handed-over March procedure**, not all earlier morning contact. The opening mentions March, so this is ambiguity rather than a proven contradiction. Naming the new feature she has not tested would avoid the feeling that four years of practice disappeared.

### 5. Choice-history sentences inherit the wrong speaker's apparent authority — low to medium

- Murata: 「現地誘導を選んだ」 (year 2, line 342)
- Physician Takagi: 「幹線を先に除雪する判断をした」 (year 2, line 1265)

These are embedded in first-person resident/doctor posts. They sound momentarily as though the restaurant owner chose the road policy and the doctor decided the snow-clearance order. The earlier field decision and actor response establish otherwise. An explicit subject (the road/administrative actor, or “the adopted proposal”) would preserve the otherwise careful proposal/execution distinction.

### 6. Summary saturation flattens voice and progress — low, pervasive

Many months have: initial problem → concrete exchange → later posts restating the same exchange → staff meeting restating it again. Across years, “do not treat this one acceptance as indefinite availability” is often stated by almost everyone. GENKAN and noodle callbacks also repeatedly use long recap sentences that sound more like archive summaries than spontaneous personal posts.

The underlying incremental changes are real, and I could track them. The cost is that new events, emotions, and decisions become a small part of a very long repeated lesson. After the disaster especially, monthly research summaries repeatedly state the same injury and business-loss status while Machi and Akio themselves have relatively few new moments. I would trim redundant restatements before adding more explanation; preserve the specific consequence, the changed condition, and ordinary-life details.

## Overall judgment

**Portable-shelter continuity on this displayed branch: clear and internally coherent at the reader level.**

**Whole sequence: understandable, with the concrete continuity/presentation reservations above.** There is no incomprehensible jump in why the shelter exists, who provides each prerequisite, or why the same equipment can be moved. The more important remaining gaps are the stale disaster-state text, the late private-scene transition, and a handful of repeated-learning/authority lines. A stronger physical deployment/relocation confirmation would improve the climax, but the current capacity/provenance distinction is already readable.
