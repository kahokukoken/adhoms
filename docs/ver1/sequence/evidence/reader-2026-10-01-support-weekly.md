# ADHOMS: independent first-reader report, normal weekly-control packet

## Result first

The initial story teaches a coherent support loop. I can explain what ADHOMS does, what the director is allowed to choose, and why the residents' replies matter, without consulting design documents or code. The first bus case is the clearest demonstration: notice an impossible timing constraint, prioritize it, investigate, propose a limited form of help, receive the person's bounded acceptance, retain the unsolved bus problem, and use that history in later years.

The supplied five-year sequence is not yet free of consequential continuity problems. Two selected choices are immediately followed by text saying the choice has not been made/confirmed. The Year 3 follow-up names a burdened group but receives a reply from a different group without explaining the connection. A mobile command post becomes available without its provider/acceptance being established in the visible history. The Year 2 decisions also lack enough current-event context to make their options well-grounded.

The final-day packet does not show the actor replies between proposals and outcomes. This is an **export-coverage limitation pending recheck**, not a certified omission in the application: after my original read, the parent reported that same-phase rerenders after choice clicks may have been omitted by the recorder. I have not invented those omitted scenes or counted them as read. The original packet alone cannot support a claim that the final-day proposal → reply → implementation loop is visible.

The longer story does preserve important distinctions: surviving is not the same as recovering one's livelihood; danger labels are not injury confirmations; recording a refusal is not loss of friendship; a prototype is not an operational warning device. The TOWA reveal is seeded and does not prematurely disclose its explanation. Repetition and sparse embodied progress are significant editorial concerns, but human enjoyment and acceptance are for human readers to judge.

## What I understand the player and ADHOMS can do

The player is Kiso, the field-trial director, not the town's mayor or an omnipotent controller. ADHOMS helps compare people's difficulties, constraints, and possible contributions. T-0WA and the staff collect, interpret, and preserve observations. The director can prioritize observations, obtain investigation material, select a proposed response, and later select what to ask about and which cooperation to develop.

The private +/− marks mean research attention, not approval/disapproval or public social reactions. They do not notify posters. Staff test posts do not initiate town investigations. Details show who is speaking and can expose a response choice. Time controls produce later posts/replies. The bus report explicitly connects the chosen + to automatic investigation. This transcript records only one +, so it demonstrates one complete operation but cannot establish how often other investigation opportunities become available.

Proposals do not themselves change a bus timetable, staff someone's shift, provide a vehicle, grant school-land use, or oblige a participant to act. People and responsible organizations may accept only part of an idea, decline it, or remain unavailable. A known contact, an old agreement, an unused shelf, and currently available operating capacity are different things.

History matters because it changes the questions that can be asked without repeating the first explanation, the people who can communicate directly, and the cooperation that may be requested. Relation is expressly not affection. Memory includes unresolved cases, limits, burden, refusal, uncertainty, expiry, and the conditions of a successful occasion. It is not permission to reuse a person's labor forever.

The culture/food choices are limited practical collaborations. The band writes its own music; Kiso can help compare listening experiences. Murata and Machi own the food work; Kiso helps test whether it can be served and cleared. These scenes successfully keep ADHOMS from becoming an automatic hit-song or restaurant-success machine.

## What the participants actually do

The residents are not passive indicators. They submit observations, explain constraints, try a proposal, negotiate its scope, decline additional work, change their own plans, and report what happened. Examples remain concrete:

- Tanaka distinguishes nursery opening, walking time, bus time, work, household calls, a one-off lift, and the other driver's postponed errands
- Haruka can report her working/commuting conditions without being made a night-time wildlife surveyor; later she identifies the writing burden of the research itself
- Kitamura keeps photo dates and arranges rice/fuel delivery, but does not become permanent patrol staff merely by having helped once
- Sakamoto opens the community hall and coordinates snow work, learns to separate opening from closing, and eventually lets successors answer for themselves
- Murata can offer rest without unlimited conversation or invisible extra labor; later she separates historical recipes from present supply commitments
- Machi has a business and family life, not an unlimited support role; Akari's play, authorship, and changing plans have their own standing
- Akio both supports the sumo event and wants to train; his time off is not spare labor, and his later injury is not erased by his survival
- Ren's prototype develops through ordinary use, handover, glove, packaging, maintenance, and instruction problems. Its non-warning status remains explicit throughout
- Minato learns handover and bounded requests, graduates, and does not convert his sister's knowledge into resident consent. Kaito starts making his own inquiries and can return decisions outside his remit
- Traffic, school, welfare, medical, road-management, and cooperating businesses act within their own responsibilities. In Year 2 and Year 4 their replies explicitly delimit implementation

The staff also change their own practice. They inspect sites, compare times, correct data categories, reduce repeated questioning, and learn not to consume a person's rest with research. The February 2034 recognition that observation itself creates burden is a convincing consequence of the preceding years.

## Blockers to claiming the supplied causal sequence is clear

“Blocker” here means a concrete obstacle to the stated narrative/choice-legibility claim. It does not certify the whole game unplayable or imply that I know the code is wrong.

### B1 — Two stale pre-choice sentences contradict choices already shown

- `year2:788`: selects garbage/food-resource management
- `year2:802`, October first Saito post: “獣害対策の選択は、この記録ではまだ確認できない”
- `year4:19`: selects deepening cooperation into agreements
- `year4:41`, April Matsumoto post: “今年の協力の進め方は、まだ判断前の記録です”

The reader has just made both choices. It is reasonable for **acceptance** still to be unknown; it is not the same as **the director's choice** being unmade/unknown. The later pending-proposal and respondent paragraphs are otherwise consistent. Keep the uncertainty but attach it to the correct stage: chosen proposal versus pending adoption. This is a text-order conflict in the original packet, irrespective of how it arose.

### B2 — Year 3 reply does not answer the named burdened group

`year3:12` selects asking the burdened side. `year3:40` quotes the prior outcome as improved welfare access with increased factory/logistics burden, then returns a **生活道路側** answer about the time people can get from their house to a main road.

That is a valid concern in this story, but the response provides no connection between that speaker and the factory/logistics burden it just named. The previous March business reply had already identified the useful next question: how much delay and when to expect it. A follow-up from that actor, or a sentence explicitly explaining the new target's relationship to the quoted burden, would close the chain. Do not silently assume the two groups are interchangeable.

### B3 — Policy choices appear without enough present-case evidence

The flood choice is inserted after May's wildlife/night-road meeting (`year2:252–264`) and before June's rain logistics story. It has a title, a disclaimer that no internal investigation is complete, and four policy options. It does not identify a present forecast, affected route, onset, urgency, or which available personnel make “onsite guidance” feasible. Last year's flood teaches why drainage matters, but does not establish why this particular response is now being selected.

The wildlife choice (`775–788`) and snow choice (`1170–1182`) likewise have seasonal background but little new event-specific decision material. Uncertainty is appropriate; absence of the basic scenario is a different limitation. A short description of known facts, unresolved facts, and whose bounded response is being requested would let a reader choose provisionally without inventing the situation. If these are advance contingency policies rather than responses to an active incident, that distinction should be explicit.

### B4 — The mobile-command capability lacks visible provenance

Year 4's received proposals list lab communications/drones/power; school grounds, contacts, and training; childcare contacts/training; warehouse opening; fuel priority. The April reply (`year4:67` onward) explicitly does **not** secure factory/logistics vehicles/personnel, while conditionally accepting the other named offers. In June the system suddenly lists **移動指令所** as available (`341`), and Saito later repeats **移動指揮**.

No preceding supplied statement establishes which organization provides this post, whether it is a vehicle or a portable setup, or what accepted offer enables it. It may be a sensible combined capability, but a first reader must currently supply that explanation. Its later selectable deployment makes this more than an ornamental noun. Give it a visible provider/combination and accepted scope, or avoid presenting it as independently secured.

The warehouse is a weaker scope-clarity concern, not the same contradiction: disaster opening was accepted in April, while Murata's routine storage/retrieval is still being negotiated in June. Those can coexist. A compact disaster-only condition beside the broad capability label would help readers keep them separate.

### B5 — Final-day response bridge unverified because of export coverage

The original final-day packet (`year5:532–730`) repeats the rule that responsible actors implement only accepted proposals. It then records selected buttons and advances to the next screen's changed predictions/risk. It does not include actual proposal-specific replies in the supplied sequence.

This produces a reading gap at precisely the point the early bus case has taught us to expect a reply. Akio's stranded injury after retained sumo-venue vehicle priority is not inherently contradictory, and “注意” was explicitly not a bodily outcome guarantee. But a reader needs the observed sequence of route failure, accepted transport availability, who remained, and the limit on reaching them to understand this particular result. The September sentence gives his being stranded when the route became unusable; it does not by itself reconstruct the response to the earlier allocation proposal.

The parent subsequently reported that actual same-phase replies may have been omitted from the recorder. Therefore this is **unverified in the UI** and should be re-read in a supplemented exact sequence before deciding that new dialogue is needed. Do not treat my original packet observation as proof that the game has no such responses.

## Non-blocking continuity and editorial concerns

1. **Small off-screen setup.** Year 1 November Tanaka says she heard Kitamura's small-container story (`year1:1162`) before the supplied sequence shows him raising it. His explanation arrives shortly afterward. This can reasonably be an off-screen conversation, so it is not a major contradiction. A light earlier mention or “相談して” formulation would remove the first-reader hiccup.

2. **Directive numbering.** Directive 2 is the first numbered directive shown in all five supplied files (`year4:21`); Directive 1 never appears. Directives 3 and 4 then follow. The principles themselves make sense, but the numbering implies a step the first reader has not seen. This could also be an export omission. I cannot infer its contents.

3. **TOWA scene's time/location label.** The private conversation follows March 2034's review, and its introduction explicitly says it happens after that review (`year5:1675`). Its title is “撤収後のステージ裏” (`1673`), while the established event stage is August's forest-park event. There is no new March performance or stage established in this packet. This is a staging ambiguity, not proof of impossible chronology; a small date/place cue would prevent the reader mentally jumping seven months backward.

4. **Unexplained final authorization/update transition.** The review describes safety as needing further verification; T-0WA then confirms update conditions and possible continued observation, and the epilogue says continued observation was approved. These can all coexist, but naming the review's actual approval decision would prevent the AI's technical statement from sounding like the source of administrative authority. This is a clarity improvement, not evidence of an unlawful act in fiction.

5. **Repetition becomes the dominant rhythm.** A typical month gives a concrete problem, the correction, a later resident paragraph summarizing that same correction, and a meeting which states the principle again. Across years the same seasonal character/issue pairing returns: bus in April, night-road/fuel in May, delivery in June, cooling in July, short help shifts in August, keys in September, rice in October, snow roster in November, year-end visitors in December, access in January, recovery/rest in February, handover in March. Progress often consists of one additional qualification. That recurrence supports memory, but it can also make substantial later changes difficult to notice.

6. **Repeated limitation language can flatten voices.** Many characters and staff repeatedly conclude “できたことと…は別”, “未確認のまま”, “次も頼めるわけではない”. These are accurate and central, but when residents, staff, and meetings all state the same rule in adjacent passages, the reader has already understood it. Preserve the person's concrete experience and retain one clear inference rather than requiring every speaker to explain the lesson.

7. **Creative threads are mostly reported as process memories.** The early GENKAN/food scenes are comparatively embodied: a lyric, a drum beat, a pot, a shelf. Later opening posts repeatedly recite the selected prior steps almost as a route ledger. The GENKAN small-live audience's exact memorable word/reaction is never as specific as the setup promised. Its five-year arc shows iteration and continuing listening more clearly than a distinct musical milestone. These are optional choices about emphasis, not contradictions or a requirement that the band become successful.

8. **Long unresolved cases need visible movement to avoid feeling reset.** The bus timetable remains under consultation for five years, grass-cutting boundaries are still being organized in Year 4, and Ren repeatedly learns another limit of the prototype. Remaining unresolved is entirely compatible with the premise. The strongest progression is in changed relationships and directly used handovers; more occasional concrete decisions, refusal reasons, or changed everyday practice would make it easier to tell deliberate long-term difficulty from stalled plot. I am not recommending false fixes to real constraints.

9. **Post-disaster named survivors become status summaries.** Machi and Akio speak in September and March, but much of the intervening continuity arrives through staff paragraphs repeating store losses/injury. That avoids silently restoring them, which is good. A few more current, bounded observations in their own voices could make the residual loss feel lived rather than audited. This is an editing preference, not a request to invent recovery or force an emotional reaction.

## Initial teaching verdict and what should be preserved

The onboarding answers the first-reader questions effectively: who is speaking, why these residents appear, why the sample is incomplete, what +/− means, which controls advance time, and what the director cannot order. The opening bus impossibility is graspable without technical vocabulary. Its delayed investigation and conditional reply show the intended loop rather than just defining it.

The material also teaches the difference between human warmth and operational availability through lunch boxes, tea, jokes, visits, music, and family time. Those small scenes are useful because people sometimes want things other than becoming more available to the system. The later “お礼だけの電話” and Akari's notebook are particularly clear payoffs.

I would preserve the bounded authority, actual return replies, continued structural bus issue, non-warning prototype limits, refusal-without-relationship-loss examples, and persistent post-disaster residuals. Correct the visible state/target/resource inconsistencies and verify the final-day display sequence before claiming end-to-end causal clarity. After that, evaluate pacing and enjoyment with human first readers; this report does not certify either.
