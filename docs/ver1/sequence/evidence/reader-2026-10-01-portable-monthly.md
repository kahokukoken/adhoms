## What I understood without outside context

### Premise and authority

I am reading as Kiso, the local field-trial research director, supported by T-0WA and four staff members. The experiment aims to preserve and adapt the functions that support life, rather than maximize population or GDP. Observation marks are private research priorities, not social approval. The opening explicitly separates proposing from accepting and executing: residents and responsible organizations retain their own decisions (year1:5–57).

The first bus story makes this concrete: nursery opens at 8:00, the stop is seven minutes away, the bus leaves at 8:05, and 8:40 is too late for work. The selected route asks Matsumoto to inquire about changing the timetable, receives a qualified response, and never misrepresents a neighbour's lift as a changed timetable. Even in the final year the original transport issue remains open.

### People and motives

- **Machi** is Kiso's childhood friend and works in the family miso shop. Feeding Kiso, supplying miso, helping Murata develop soba, and protecting time with her younger sister Akari make her an actual person before her shop is lost. Her final hesitation over vats, accounts, and family follows those established priorities.
- **Akio** is another childhood friend, with sumo/competition and event-operation commitments. His tendency to stay, physical fatigue, efforts to protect training, and gradual handover of roles prepare the final risk of remaining at the sumo site. ## Findings requiring attention

### 1. Missing prerequisite: which secured vehicles are being allocated?

**Priority: substantive continuity/decision clarity.**

Year4:133–136 explicitly refuses factory/logistics vehicle/personnel provision and states that missing transport prevents portable shelter preparation. Year5:486–490 and every later final-day resource panel preserve the missing-vehicle condition. However:

- year5:545–547 accepts transport priority within a **prior transport agreement**
- year5:582–584 accepts priority allocation of **secured vehicles**
- year5:659–685 describes a finite shared evacuation-vehicle pool, allows its redistribution, and accepts keeping it in place

Those later explanations correctly make clear that reallocating does not manufacture extra vehicles. They do not establish where the original pool came from. I cannot determine whether this is an actual inconsistent resource grant or a separate ordinary municipal/event transport pool that is merely unnamed. The shown story needs one concrete distinction: who has supplied the ordinary evacuation vehicles, and why those vehicles/personnel cannot serve the shelter/mobile-command requirement. Do not infer that distinction from implementation on the reader's behalf.

## Pacing and presentation observations

These are not claims of broken state.

- The monthly sequence commonly gives an opening problem, an intermediate/week-four report, and a staff meeting that paraphrases both. Across five years the same “being able to contact is not being able to act,” “one success is not future consent,” “a blank is not availability” distinctions recur very often. The annual development is real, but repeated restatement can make it feel slower than the underlying changes. The useful progression is: discover conditions → test contact/acceptance → reveal shifted work → hand over authority and refusal → test those relations under disaster and recovery. More meetings could concentrate on what changed, rather than restate the entire distinction.
- The strongest small scenes are embodied: plates obstruct the next dish, a cable nearly gets pulled during rehearsal, someone cannot turn a page in gloves, Sakamoto cannot help blurting out the answer, Akari insists playtime is not cleaning time. These make the repeated principles feel like lived experience.
- Year4:136 puts six organizations and the shelter refusal into one very long response paragraph. Its distinctions are correct but the critical refused vehicle prerequisite is easy to lose. Separate organization-labelled responses would improve the reader's ability to retain the resource chain.
- The final day sharply changes from resident/staff dialogue into repeated institutional confirmations and metric panels. The pre-crisis July scenes successfully establish the three places and motives, but the actual danger and rescue outcomes are mostly summarized. I can follow the sequence; I feel the people less directly during it than before or after.
- “現在の選択に基づく見通し” and explicit risk-versus-confirmed-injury labels help avoid a false inference that a risk badge is a death verdict. However, the 9-minute delay/8-hour logistics figures and final scores have no visible baseline or scale in these files. I understand the qualitative outcome, not why a particular number has that size. That is a limitation of this text-only assessment, not proof that no other UI supplies it.
- Year5:1556 says “月次記録：11か月” despite 12 calendar headings. August is visibly the final-day sequence, so coverage is understandable, but adding “豪雨当日は別記録” would prevent an apparent missing-month worry.

## Continuities that worked and should be preserved

- The characters are introduced before their central uses; no late person is needed to repair a missing plot prerequisite. Kaito's label is enough for his limited role.
- The bus's initial unsolved timetable problem remains separate from later social coordination. The story does not retroactively claim that the chosen timetable inquiry improved the actual bus timetable.
- Year-two proposals visibly wait for qualified organizational acceptance. Later years revisit the selected policies rather than quietly substituting another branch.
- Lab-maintained communications/power are expressly distinguished from Ren's unsafe-for-warning prototypes (year4:136 versus year5:266–340). There is no visible claim that his hobby prototype becomes trusted emergency infrastructure just because it exists.
- The shelter refusal is explicit and stable. School-ground access is not equated with portable shelter equipment; warehouse space is not equated with a truck; a non-deployed shelter's night status-quo response explicitly says it remains non-deployed (year5:636–644).
- Akio's lower on-screen risk after sumo-focused vehicles does not promise no injury. The later statement that he was injured after route failure is compatible with the displayed warning that risk is not an injury/death determination.
- Recovery does not silently restore Machi's business or Akio's athletic availability. The distinction between survival, restored livelihood, and aggregate evaluation is maintained through the last conversation.
- Minato's labels track school years and graduation without falsely promoting him to an administrative official. Aoi's and Ren's future paths remain broad/unfinished, but I never needed an unseen job appointment to understand their actions.

## Bottom line

**Qualified narrative pass for this one monthly, shelter-unavailable sequence.** The overall story, selected choices, relationships, and recovery consequences are readable without design knowledge. Resolve or explicitly distinguish the final-day vehicle prerequisite; clarify historical versus live hazard text at convergence; tighten the few reset-like lines and the resource-response presentation. Successful portable-shelter preparation/deployment/repositioning remains **unreviewed** because it never occurs in the supplied route.
